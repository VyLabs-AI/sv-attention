\section{The implicit vector--Jacobian product}\label{app:vjp}
This section describes the custom backward for the fp64 maintained solver; it is
not the backward used by the stabilized batched training path. Within a fixed
active-set partition $(S, E, R)$ the free variables $u = [\rho;\, \alpha_S]$
satisfy the bordered KKT system $M u = c$, with $M = [[0, \mathbf{1}^\top],[\mathbf{1},
2K_{SS}]]$ and
\begin{equation}
c = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - |E|\,C \\ \mathrm{diag}(K)_S - 2C\, K_{SE}\mathbf{1} \end{bmatrix},
\qquad u = \mathcal{R} c, \quad \mathcal{R} = M^{-1}.
\end{equation}
For a loss $L$ with upstream cotangents $\bar\rho, \bar\alpha_S$, let
$\bar u = [\bar\rho;\, \bar\alpha_S]$ and $w = \mathcal{R}^\top \bar u$ with margin block
$w_S$. Differentiating $u = \mathcal{R} c$ and using $d\mathcal{R} = -\mathcal{R}\, dM\,
\mathcal{R}$ gives $dL = w^\top dc - w^\top dM\, u$; collecting the coefficients of
$dK_{ij}$,
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial L}{\partial K_{ii}} &\mathrel{+}= [w_S]_i && (i \in S), &
\frac{\partial L}{\partial K_{ie}} &\mathrel{+}= -2C\,[w_S]_i && (i \in S,\, e \in E), &
\frac{\partial L}{\partial K_{ij}} &\mathrel{+}= -2\,[w_S]_i\,[\alpha_S]_j && (i, j \in S).
\end{align}
Every term is a rank-one assembly, and the custom backward only multiplies by
$\mathcal{R}^\top$, which the maintained forward pass already stores; it performs no
second system solve. Changes in the active set happen on a measure-zero set of inputs
across which the map is piecewise smooth; as with the kink in a ReLU, we do not route
gradient through the discrete change in set membership. The expressions are verified
against finite differences by double-precision \texttt{gradcheck}. This
inverse-reuse/no-second-solve statement does not apply to Appendix~\ref{app:solver}.

\section{The batched projected-gradient solver}\label{app:solver}
The language-model training forward uses a stabilized approximation for every
(sequence, head, chunk) problem. A sequential active-set walk solves one problem at a
time; instead, the single-precision accelerated projected gradient of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:fista} estimates all partitions at once. Because \eqref{eq:svdd} is a
box- and sum-constrained convex quadratic program, each FISTA step is a dense batched
matrix product and a vectorized projection onto the capped simplex
$\{a : \sum_i a_i = 1,\, 0 \le a_i \le C\}$, computed by bisection on the dual
variable. The projection and step are fused into one compiled graph and run through
MLX~\citep{mlx}; a per-row mask lets problems of different prefix length share one
batch.

The estimated coefficients define masks $(S,E)$. In PyTorch, the implementation packs
their bordered KKT systems, adds a fixed ridge $\lambda=10^{-3}$ to each valid margin
diagonal, and computes
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{M}u=c,\qquad
\widetilde{M}=M+\lambda\,\mathrm{diag}(0,\mathbf{1}_S),
\qquad \widetilde{\alpha}_S=u_S
\end{equation}
with \texttt{torch.linalg.solve}. Standard autograd differentiates this solve. Thus
$\widetilde{\alpha}$ is the exact solution of the \emph{ridged packed system}, but only
a stabilized approximation to the unregularized coefficients of \eqref{eq:svdd}. This
path neither invokes the custom VJP of Appendix~\ref{app:vjp} nor inherits its
no-second-solve property, and it is not used for the fixed-$C$
decrement/refit certificate.

\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Stabilized batched SVDD training approximation (one FISTA step shown; all
problems in parallel).}
\label{alg:fista}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\State \textbf{input} Gram tensors $K \in \mathbb{R}^{G \times n \times n}$, box $C$, mask
$\mu \in \{0,1\}^{G \times n}$
\State $L \gets 2\,\lambda_{\max}(K)$ \Comment{Lipschitz constant; batched power iteration}
\State $a \gets \mu / \!\sum \mu,\; y \gets a,\; t \gets 1$
\For{$k = 1, \dots, T$}
  \State $g \gets 2 K y - \mathrm{diag}(K)$ \Comment{batched gradient}
  \State $a' \gets \Pi_{\triangle_C}\!\big(y - g / L,\, \mu\big)$ \Comment{capped-simplex projection (bisection)}
  \State $t' \gets \tfrac{1}{2}\big(1 + \sqrt{1 + 4 t^2}\big),\quad
         y \gets a' + \tfrac{t - 1}{t'}(a' - a)$
  \State $a \gets a',\; t \gets t'$
\EndFor
\State derive estimated masks $(S,E)$ from $a$
\State $\widetilde M \gets M + 10^{-3}\,\mathrm{diag}(0,\mathbf{1}_S)$
\State $u \gets \texttt{torch.linalg.solve}(\widetilde M,c)$
       \Comment{autograd differentiates the ridged solve}
\State \textbf{return} stabilized $\widetilde{\alpha}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Experimental details}\label{app:details}
\paragraph{Models and training.} The backbone is a pre-norm decoder with token and learned
positional embeddings, a weight-tied output head, and a pluggable attention module so every
variant shares an identical backbone. The $3.22$M configuration is $d_{\text{model}}{=}256$,
$4$ layers, $4$ heads, block $256$; the $10$M configuration is $d_{\text{model}}{=}384$, $6$
layers, $6$ heads, block $512$. All variants train with AdamW ($\beta = (0.9, 0.95)$, weight
decay $0.1$), gradient clip $1.0$, a cosine schedule, and batch $16$; we report
the best validation BPC over training. The \(3.22\)M runs use the first
\(5{,}000{,}000\) enwik8 bytes, while the \(10\)M and \(32\)M runs use the first
\(20{,}000{,}000\), each with a $90/10$ train/validation split and byte
vocabulary \(256\). The primitive solver hyperparameter is the box $C$. The
language-model runs set $C=0.25$ directly.
In studies that report $\nu$, we calibrate $C:=1/(\nu n_0)$ once at the declared
admission/reference size $n_0$, record that scalar, and do not recompute it as the
context size changes. Because the constrained problem requires $nC\geq1$, the causal
implementation uses an ungated warm-up below $\lceil1/C\rceil+2$ available keys and
issues no SV certificate there. The gate uses an RBF kernel, chunk length $64$, and
$80$ FISTA iterations in the batched solver.

The fixed-\(C\) decrement/refit audit and certificates on frozen learned keys
use the maintained CPU solver in fp64. The fixed-context synthetic and clinical
selection benchmarks instead solve independent batch QPs and use their support
sets as point-in-time selection policies; they are not maintained-decrement
audits. The language-model training and throughput paths use the
single-precision batched partition finder followed by the \(10^{-3}\)-ridged
\texttt{torch.linalg.solve}/autograd path; their coefficients are a stabilized
training approximation. When a deletion certificate is reported on a trained
model, its frozen learned keys are re-solved by the fp64 maintained solver.
Clinical experiments use MIMIC-IV under its credentialed data use agreement.
For the held-out-channel control, the event label is unstandardized hourly
SpO\(_2<90\) after within-stay forward/backward filling and cohort-median
imputation; the standardized SpO\(_2\) column is removed before the
five-dimensional gate and all matched-budget baselines are computed. The
reported stay-level percentile interval bootstraps the \(718\) paired per-stay
retention differences \(10{,}000\) times with seed \(0\), treating stays as
independent resampling units.

\paragraph{Throughput protocol.} We run
\verb|python -m experiments.g1_throughput --paper| on an Apple M3 Ultra
(32 physical CPU cores, 512\,GiB unified memory), macOS 26.5, Python 3.11.14,
PyTorch 2.12.1, NumPy 2.4.6, and MLX 0.31.2. A timed step is
\texttt{zero\_grad} plus full-model forward, cross-entropy, and backward; it excludes
the optimizer update, data loading, evaluation, and checkpointing. Direct rows use
batch $16$ and report the median and interquartile range of seven separately
synchronized samples after three warm-ups. The mixed SV row includes PyTorch CPU
work, host/device transfers, MLX-GPU FISTA, the ridged PyTorch KKT solve, and
backward. The two exact active-set diagnostics implement the RBF readout, are
measured at batch $2$, and are linearly extrapolated by $8\times$; they are retained
as historical diagnostics rather than like-for-like hybrid comparisons. Softmax is
the repository's unfused masked implementation, not FlashAttention.

\paragraph{Matched-state protocol.} For each seed we train the SV hybrid, measure its mean
active state $|S \cup E|$ on held-out data, and set the sliding-window Transformer's window to
that state plus the local-window length, so the two layers see a matched token budget. The
recurrent baselines (DeltaNet, linear attention) carry their own fixed state and are reported
at it; full softmax is the unbounded reference. The seven-seed paired comparison is only
SV versus matched swa; the DeltaNet, linear, and full-softmax rows are single-seed
references. Every variant uses the identical backbone, optimizer, schedule, and training
token budget.

\paragraph{Fixed-\(C\) decrement/refit protocol.} For each trial we build a
context, set \(C\) once, solve the gate in fp64, and remove a token two ways:
(i) the C\&P reverse path and (ii) a retained-set refit under that same \(C\).
Recomputing \(C\) from the smaller set would define a different problem. We
compare their decision functions
$f(x) = 2\sum_i \alpha_i \kappa(x, x_i) - \rho$ on probes spanning the retained, forgotten,
and jittered points, and report the maximum deviation. We use the decision function rather
than the raw coefficients because on exact duplicates the SVDD optimum is non-unique
($\alpha$ and the partition may differ while the function is identical), so the function is
the correct equivalence test. The decay baseline scales the forgotten token's coefficient by $\gamma$ and
is evaluated on the same metric. Regimes are Gaussian keys (\(d=6\)),
redundant near-duplicates, real MIMIC-IV hourly vitals, and a trained model's
learned key projections; \(300\) attempts each. The report stores all
\(1{,}200\) statuses: \(1{,}199\) complete and one MIMIC decrement reaches the
explicit margin-empty edge case.

\section{Per-seed matched-state results}\label{app:seeds}
Table~\ref{tab:seeds} gives the per-seed bits-per-character for the SV hybrid and the
matched-state sliding-window Transformer (swa) at $3.22$M parameters, the basis for the
significance test in Section~\ref{sec:quality}. Every one of the seven seeds favors the
hybrid. Mean BPC is $2.178$ for SV and $2.383$ for swa; the mean paired relative
improvement is $8.6\%$ ($95\%$ \(t\)-interval $[5.2, 12.0]\%$). A two-sided
paired \(t\)-test on the absolute BPC differences gives $t = 5.95$
($p = 0.00101$); a Wilcoxon signed-rank test gives $p = 0.016$,
and Cohen $d_z = 2.25$.

On the contemporary TinyStories corpus~\citep{tinystories} (also $3.22$M,
byte-level), the hybrid has lower BPC on all three seeds:
$1.329/1.185/1.269$ versus matched swa $1.434/1.451/1.526$, a mean improvement
of $14.2\%$ (paired $t=4.0$, $p=0.057$ at $n=3$). The result is directionally
positive but non-significant, not statistical confirmation.

\paragraph{Larger scales.} At $10$M parameters (block $512$, five seeds) the
matched-state comparison reverses at the fixed \(6{,}000\)-step budget:
per-seed SV vs.\ swa best-validation BPC is $2.406/2.502$, $2.249/2.661$,
$2.415/2.426$, $2.475/2.286$, $3.127/2.524$, a mean of $+2.5\%$ (SV worse)
with a standard deviation of ${\sim}15\%$. Several best values occur at or near
the final evaluations, and the hybrid traces remain high-variance, so this is a
matched-budget rather than a matched-convergence comparison. In one \(32\)M run
(\(d_{\rm model}=512\), \(10\) layers, \(8\) heads), the hybrid reaches
\(4.456\) BPC while still improving late in training, versus \(3.106\) for swa
at the same step budget. These runs are consistent with slower, less stable
optimization of the chunk-frozen gate; whether the ordering persists at
matched convergence remains open
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).
