\begin{abstract}
Auditable memory requires more than a small state: it requires a statement of
which output is preserved, relative to which reference solve, and across which
updates. We introduce Support Vector Attention, whose one-class gate exposes
zero-coefficient reserve tokens and a maintained active set, enabling explicit
contracts not exposed by an undifferentiated dense state. We prove that
removing a reserve token without re-solving preserves the solved context's
readout, and that a completed active-token decrement targets the retained-key
coefficient optimum at the same box parameter \(C\). Across \(1{,}200\) fp64
decrement/refit trials on Gaussian, redundant, real MIMIC-IV, and learned keys,
\(1{,}199\) complete; regime-level median finite-probe decision-function
deviations range from \(4.5\times10^{-13}\) to \(5.7\times10^{-7}\), and
reference-run median deletion/refit speedups are \(24\)--\(223\times\) on the
reference CPU. We also characterize the boundary of the reserve certificate:
a deterministic construction proves that current reserve status alone cannot
guarantee full-history equivalence after arbitrary future admissions. At
matched budgets, support selection yields held-out rare-group query recall
\(0.861\) versus \(0.319\) for an oracle H2O-style selector under skewed
redundancy. In a held-out-channel ICU control that removes direct label-channel
reuse, SpO\(_2\) alone defines events and is excluded from all selector inputs;
threshold-hour retention is \(0.464\) versus \(0.225\) for an H2O-style
self-mass selector. A separate batched path supports
end-to-end training; at \(3.22\)M parameters, seven paired seeds yield mean best
validation BPC \(2.178\) versus \(2.383\) (two-sided paired \(t\)-test,
\(p=0.001\)). The certificates are point-in-time and fixed-\(C\) by
construction; future-safe streaming and general-purpose performance remain
open, and larger fixed-budget runs are consistent with slower optimization.
\end{abstract}
